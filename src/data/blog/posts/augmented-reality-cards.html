What are Augmented Reality (AR) Cards?

Augmented reality cards are actual physical cards adorned with digital content that are viewed using an AR-enabled device (phone, tablet, or computer). The image of the card serves as a visual reminder (cue), while at the same time, the AR technology superimposes videos, animations, 3D models, audio, buttons, or interactive elements on the real-world image.

AR cards can be utilized in business, training, marketing, greetings, events, games, travel, and collectibles. They link a more immersive digital experience with a physical card, but do not require physical removal of the card.

How Do Augmented Reality Cards Work?

An augmented reality card features a printed image, marker, or other visual stimulus that can be identified by a camera, which then displays additional content over the top of it. As soon as it has been identified, AR software displays the proper digital content on or around the card via the device screen.

The overall steps are:

  • Users launch an AR app or support WebAR experience.

  • The card is physically scanned by the camera.

  • The AR software identifies the image/marker.

  • This system loads the digital content that is linked.

  • Video, models, animation, sounds, and interactions are presented on screen.

  • The user may watch, move, tap, or interact with the content.

This experience will vary depending on the tracking method, device, and nature of the AR content designed for the card.

How to Make Augmented Reality Cards?

The components of building augmented reality cards are a physical card design, digital content, tracking, and an AR platform or custom-written application to tie the pieces together. It begins with defining what it needs to do, moves to creation of the card, then testing, and finally its launch. 

Step 1: Decide the Purpose of the AR Card

First, determine what happens if a card is scanned. The objective could be to display a product, include contact information, play a personal message, explain a concept, act in an AR card game, or promote an event.

Once you've got a clear purpose, the rest of the process will be easier. If you're going for an AR business card, you might want to add a portfolio and contacts button, and if you're going for an education card, you might want to add labels and explanations to a 3D model. 

Step 2: Design the Physical Card

Design and create the printed design that the user will use to scan. The appearance of the card should be pleasing to the eye, yet there should be a sufficient level of detail on the card for the AR system to identify it accurately.

Improved image tracking for good contrast, clear images, and distinct visual features. On the card, there should also be simple instructions like ‘Scan to view in AR' as this will alert users right away that an AR experience is available. 

Step 3: Create the Digital AR Content

Then, design the content to be seen when the card is scanned. This can contain video, audio, animation, 3D-models, text, images, interactive buttons, as well as game elements.

The content should directly meet the purpose of the card. For one, a 3D demo could show on a product card; for another, a feature of Santa related to Christmas could come to life in an AR Christmas card. 

Step 4: Choose the AR Tracking Method

Figure out how the physical card will be detected by the AR system. One of the most frequently used applications of image tracking is with printed designs to align digital content around the printed piece

The use of marker-based tracking is also possible if a clear visual marker is present on a card. This will depend on the card design, the number of cards that are to be employed, the desired accuracy, where the cards will be used, and the complexity of the experience. 

Step 5: Choose WebAR or an AR App

Determine access to the AR experience. Enables having compatible experiences open in mobile browsers, potentially eliminating the need for an app download.

For cards that fit in a larger application or need more advanced features, user accounts, saved progress, deeper integration with the device, etc., a dedicated AR app might be more ideal. The decision must be made with consideration of the audience and the technical needs. 

Step 6: Connect the Physical Card to the AR Experience

After creating the card and digital content, link them up via an AR system. The Software should be able to identify the right card and retrieve the corresponding digital experience.

For instance, when you scan a standing card of a product in print, you may find a 3D model of the same product, surrounding information, and a button to go to a product/book or ordering page. The two components must sync together; it is a combined experience. 

Step 7: Test the AR Card on Different Devices

Test the finalized card before launch on various mobile devices, screen sizes, OS versions, and browsers. Test the card in various lighting situations, camera orientations, and distances also.

Testing should be performed on the actual print card and not the digital design. This helps to identify problems with glare, print quality, tracking, page turn time, unclear instructions, etc., before undertaking a larger print run. 

Step 8: Print, Launch and Measure Engagement

Once testing is finished, print the final cards and start the AR experience. Include analytics in the system and monitor use of the cards after deployment.

It can be useful metrics such as scans, repeat visits, video views, button clicks, session length, video product visits, and completed actions. Learning from these experiences has the potential to enhance the AR experience for future iterations of the system.

Augmented Reality Cards vs QR Code Cards

QR code cards and augmented reality cards are both types of physical-to-digital connections, but they do it in different ways.

A QR code is primarily designed to take users to any website, landing page, video, form, or other digital location. Using the device camera, AR overlays an interactive digital media experience over the physical card the user is looking at.

What Makes a Good AR Card Experience?

AR cards should be easy to open and load quickly, and be easy to understand. The digital material should not simply be designed to add effects, but to be a valuable addition. 

Strong Image Recognition

The AR system must also be easily identified, promptly and reliably. The clearer the image, the better the contrast, the more visual details, and the higher the quality of the printing, the easier tracking will be. 

Fast Loading

The load time of the AR content should be fast on mobile devices. Large 3D models, videos, and animations are best optimized so that users will not leave until they have the experience. 

Useful Interactive Content

All digital components should have a clear purpose. The information should enable users to learn, explore, contact, purchase, watch, play, or perform some other helpful action. 

Clear Instructions for Users

All of the users should know immediately how to enter the AR experience. Simple instructions like "Scan to view in AR" can help minimize confusion and encourage participation. 

Mobile-Friendly AR

AR cards should be easily readable on the smartphone display. Content such as text and buttons, controls, animations, and 3D content should always be readily seen and usable on supported devices.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)



How Long Do Augmented Reality Cards Last?

The time-to-life of augmented reality cards is not predetermined. The physical card can last as long as it’s readable, and the digital can persist as long as the necessary app, site, hosting, and AR platform are available. 



Can the AR Content Be Updated After the Cards Are Printed?

Yes, in many AR setups. If you are trying to track the printed card, there is no requirement to reprint if the contents are controlled via digital technologies like an updated video, 3D model, link, or other content.



Do AR Cards Work on Both Android and iPhone?

AR cards can be used on Android and iPhone devices, but compatibility will be dependent on the AR technology used, browser, app, and device model. It is significant to test on the targeted devices before release. 



Do Augmented Reality Cards Need an Internet Connection?

Not always. There are some AR experiences available through apps that can be locally stored. WebARs and experiences that pull in content, analytics, or live data typically require an Internet connection. 



Can Multiple AR Experiences Be Added to One Card?

Yes. One page or card may contain multiple digital components like video, 3D models, buttons, audio, animation, and interactive features, etc. The experience should not be too complicated to be understood by the user.



Can AR Cards Track User Engagement and Interactions?

Yes, if analytics are included in the AR experience. Enterprises can monitor scans, sessions, clicks, video views, visits to links, and more. 



What File Formats Can Be Used for AR Card Content?

Supported file formats may vary from an AR platform or development framework. Content types include 3D graphics, animations, audio, images, video, and text. 



Can Augmented Reality Cards Be Used at Large Events and Exhibitions?

Yes. AR cards can be used in conferences, trade shows, exhibitions, product launches, museums, or brand events. For larger projects, the Internet connection and how compliant devices will be used and by whom should also be taken into account. 



Are AR Cards Suitable for Mass Printing?

Yes. When you have all the cards with the same AR feature, this same design can be printed in big batches. For projects that require any content that is different from one card to the next, then a more complex technical setup may be required. 



Can Augmented Reality Cards Be Integrated With CRM or Marketing Platforms?

Yes. When appropriate, custom AR card experiences could be linked to CRM systems, analytics, booking systems, lead forms, and websites, as well as other marketing systems via appropriate APIs and integrations.
